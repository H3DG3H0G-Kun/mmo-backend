# The Lore Bible — Cosmology of *Watcher* (მცველი)

> The settled answer to the one open question the design left: **why is memory fraying,
> and who/what is the Watcher?** This is canon. Tales extend it; they never contradict it.
> Companion docs: [GAME_DESIGN.md](../GAME_DESIGN.md) (the systems), this file (the soul),
> and the per-tale docs under [docs/lore/tales/](tales/).

---

## 0. The one sentence

**What is truly remembered is not lost — and the Watcher is the soul that keeps the watch
until Georgian memory is whole again.**

Everything below serves that sentence.

---

## 1. The single metaphysical claim (and the line we never cross)

This game makes **exactly one** claim about how its world works:

> **Remembrance is holy and sustaining. What is witnessed truly, and held in love, endures.
> What is forgotten frays out of the world.**

That is the whole cosmology. Notice what it deliberately does **not** do: it never names the
God of the world, never ranks the faiths, never declares whose heaven is real. This is a
conscious design law, because *Watcher* must be a homecoming for a believing Orthodox Georgian,
for a lover of the old pagan myth, and for a secular patriot — **all at once, none insulted.**

How the single claim lands for each:

- **For the Orthodox player** it reads, unmistakably, as **საუკუნო ხსოვნა — Memory Eternal**:
  the Church's own dismissal hymn (*„ხსენებაჲ საუკუნოჲ"*), the communion of saints, the
  conviction that the departed are held in eternal remembrance before God. The game leans on
  Christian Georgia's own treasures of memory-and-light — Saint Nino, the Robe, Svetitskhoveli,
  the living pillar — and treats them with full reverence.
- **For the myth-lover** the pre-Christian ages keep their *own* gods — Armazi, Morige Ghmerti,
  Dali, Amirani — witnessed as *what that age believed*, with respect, never mocked and never
  asserted by the game as the truth of the cosmos. The Watcher witnesses belief; the engine
  never adjudicates it.
- **For everyone** the unifying image is neutral and cultural, not doctrinal: **light**, the
  turning sun (**ბორჯღალი / Borjgali**) as a *symbol* of continuance. Borjgali already lives
  beside the Bolnisi cross in Georgian heraldry and ornament — it carries no theological claim,
  so it is safe as our visual and sonic grammar.

**The line we never cross:** the game never says the old gods were real *or* false, never stages
a miracle as "proven," never makes the Church a faction or a villain, never puts a saint or a
holy figure in danger of being "lost" by the player's failure in a way that would trivialize
faith. We depict belief; we do not arbitrate it. (See §6, Sensitivity rules.)

---

## 2. The fraying — მივიწყება (Mivitsqeba), the Unremembering

Memory frays because **forgetting is real**. We name the force **მივიწყება (Mivitsqeba)** —
*the Unremembering*, the simple, rising dark of being forgotten. It is rendered as **ნისლი
(nisli) — a mist** that eats the edges of what happened.

Crucially, **Mivitsqeba is not a devil.** It is not Satan, not a rival god, not evil with a
throne — naming it so would be a theological claim, and we don't make those. It is *entropy
with a Georgian face*: the burned library, the broken oral chain, the fortress a conqueror
renames, the poem whose meaning is twisted until no one remembers what it meant. Every real
erasure in Georgian history — and there are centuries of them — is, in the fiction, Mivitsqeba
pressing in. That is what makes the theme land for a nation that has been nearly erased and
has, every time, *remembered itself back into being.*

When the Unremembering needs a **hand** — an agent with will, a thing you can actually fight —
it works through the **ქაჯნი (Kajni)**: the sorcerer-spirits of Georgian folklore and of
Rustaveli's own **Kajeti**. The Kajni do not forget passively; they **falsify** — they re-knot
the thread of memory into a lie. This closes a beautiful loop: the Kajni you fight across the
whole game are the same Kajni the *Knight in the Panther's Skin* names. (Distortion taxonomy: §5.)

## 3. The medium — the Sun-Thread (and why the engine already calls it a "Thread")

Memory runs through time like a **thread of light** — imagined as hanging from the turning
**Borjgali**. Where the thread is tended, an era is bright and whole; where it frays, the era
goes dim and people half-forget they ever had a king, a poem, a name of their own.

This is not decoration bolted on — it is *already your mechanic*. The narrative engine's unit
is literally a **Thread**. The **Living Timeline (ცოცხალი მატიანე)** that the whole server
re-illuminates, era by era, **is the Sun-Thread being relit.** The cosmology and the build are
the same object seen from two sides. Restoration *looks like* and *sounds like* light returning.

## 4. The Watcher — მცველი (Mtsveli)

**Who you are.** At the threshold between life and rest, your soul was offered the road home —
and was instead **given a watch.** You did not refuse heaven; you *accepted a calling*: to walk
back up the years and keep the memory of the homeland until it is whole. This is framed as a
**vocation, not a rebellion** — closer to a monastic vow or a sentinel's oath than to a ghost
denied peace. You are not cursed. You are *entrusted.* The Georgian word is exact: **მცველი**,
*the one who watches, the guardian* — and it is the game's own name.

**What you may do, and never do.** You do not rewrite history or slay its heroes. You
**remember, witness, and restore** (the five Interactions: Witness / Aid / Ward / Choose /
Restore — see GAME_DESIGN §4). You stand *defensively of the story.* The most sacred Beats are
pure **Witness**, because in this world the act of true seeing is itself an act of keeping.

**Why a soul that does not die.** Immortality here is not power fantasy — it is *burden and
love.* You outlive everyone you witness; you carry them. That is the emotional engine of the
whole game, and it is why the dominant verbs are *remember / restore / protect*, never
*conquer.* (This deliberately mirrors **Amirani**, the bound immortal who suffers for the
people — a kinship the myth-strand will make explicit.)

**Left for the player to fill.** Your *name, your face, the life you lived before the watch* are
yours. The game gives you the vocation; it never forces a backstory over you. You are every
Georgian who ever lit a candle for an ancestor, given one form — and also, specifically, you.

## 5. Distortions — the bosses, without disrespect

Per GAME_DESIGN §8, a Distortion is a generic encounter bound to a Beat, **never the beloved
figure.** Our canon taxonomy, from faintest to most willful:

- **გამოძახილი (Gamodzakhili) — Echo.** A hollow repetition of a force or event: a siege that
  will not end, a storm that keeps remembering a tragedy. No will of its own.
- **ჩრდილი / ასპექტი (Chrdili / Aspect).** A single quality torn loose and grown monstrous —
  *the Aspect of Vengeance*, *the Aspect of the Foreign Yoke*, *the Panther's grief.* Symbolic,
  never the literal character.
- **ქაჯი (Kaji) — the falsifier.** The Unremembering given will: actively bends a true memory
  into a lie. The "boss with agency," and the through-line to Rustaveli's Kajeti.

The reward of victory is **narrative**: the tale is held true. Combat is *Ward* — protecting the
memory — not slaughter.

## 6. Sensitivity rules (binding on all authored content)

These are hard constraints, equal in force to "never kill a historical/literary figure":

1. **No theological verdicts.** The game never declares a faith true or false, never stages a
   miracle as mechanically "proven," never makes salvation a game state. Belief is *witnessed*,
   not adjudicated.
2. **The Church is never a faction or a villain.** No Orthodox institution, saint, hierarch, or
   sacrament is ever an antagonist, a loot source, or a "fail state." Saint Nino, Svetitskhoveli,
   and all holy figures are treated with the reverence the real culture gives them.
3. **Sacred figures are never in *mechanical* peril from the player.** A Distortion threatens the
   *memory* of a holy moment, never the holy person as a kill-target or escort-fail. The Watcher
   *wards*; failure dims a memory, it does not desecrate a saint.
4. **Pagan myth is honored as belief, not endorsed as cosmos.** Armazi, the devis, Dali, Amirani
   appear as the living faith of their age — depicted with dignity, never as "the real gods of
   the world" and never as demons to be mocked. We show; we don't rank.
5. **Georgian text is primary.** The original ქართული verse/chronicle is the canon; translations
   are secondary aids (GAME_DESIGN §6).
6. **Chronological and source honesty.** Where a tradition is *tradition* (e.g. the chronicle's
   claim that Parnavaz created the script), the design doc says so plainly. We honor the memory
   *as the source records it*, and we're honest about what is chronicle and what is history.

## 8. How this holds every Georgian story (the closing argument)

- A **chronicle** (Parnavaz's founding) is a Saga of True Threads where Mivitsqeba would leave
  the land nameless and letterless, and the Watcher *witnesses the name and the letters into
  permanence.*
- A **legend** (Amirani) is a myth-strand Saga where the people's *remembrance* is literally what
  keeps the immortal's chains from breaking — the cosmology made into a quest.
- A **poem** (სტუმარ-მასპინძელი, ვეფხისტყაოსანი) is a Saga whose Threads are its chapters, opened
  where the work lives, witnessed in its real ქართული verse, warded against the Kajni who would
  twist its meaning, and kept forever in the Watcher's Matiane.

One claim — *what is truly remembered is not lost.* Three strands. Every faith honored, none
asserted. Infinite content, zero engine rewrites, total respect. **This is the soul. Now we
author the memory, one tale at a time.**
