## 1. Why this tale — the Watcher's own mirror

**Amirani** is the Georgian Prometheus: the გმირი who defied the deity, was chained to the
Caucasus, and is eternally gnawed — and whom the people's **remembrance literally sustains.** The
folk belief is exact and astonishing: the smiths' **hammering on Holy Thursday renews Amirani's
chains**, and were the people to *forget*, his bonds would break and the world unmake. That is our
entire cosmology told as a single myth — *what is remembered is held; what is forgotten frays
loose.* Amirani is the bound immortal who suffers for humanity; the Watcher is the deathless soul
who carries memory. They are the same shape. This is the truest MYTH-strand keystone we have.

## 2. Honest myth (the core, across variants)

- **Amirani**, often with his brothers **ბადრი (Badri)** and **უსიფი (Usip)**, performs the
  great deeds — slaying **devis** and the **გველეშაპი (gveleshapi, dragon)**, winning **ქამარი
  (Kamari)** from the sky/sea power.
- His pride sets him against **ღმერთი (Ghmerti)**; he is bound in chains to the Caucasus (Mt.
  Elbrus/Khvamli in variants), an eagle/bird tearing at him, a faithful dog (**ყურша**) licking
  the chain thin.
- Each year the chain nearly breaks — and is **renewed by the smiths' hammering**, the people's
  remembrance holding him. (We honor the variant tradition as tradition — see §5.)

## 3. The Distortion

**The Unremembering itself, as a creeping Forgetting (გამოძახილი / Echo + the encroaching Nisli).**
Not a monster Amirani must be saved *from* — the Distortion is the *world's forgetting of him*,
which would let the chains break and the deep memory unravel. The Watcher **wards remembrance** —
stands with the smiths, keeps the myth alive — rather than freeing or fighting the titan.
